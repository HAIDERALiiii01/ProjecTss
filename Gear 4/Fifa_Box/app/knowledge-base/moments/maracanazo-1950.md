# The Maracanazo (1950)

## What Happened
In the deciding match of the 1950 World Cup's final round-robin group, hosts Brazil needed only a draw against Uruguay at the newly built Estádio do Maracanã to win their first title in front of what remains one of the largest crowds ever to watch a football match. Uruguay won 2-1, with Alcides Ghiggia scoring the winning goal.

## Why It Matters
The result, forever known in Brazil as the "Maracanazo" (the Maracanã Blow), is considered one of the greatest shocks in football history and one of the most traumatic moments in Brazilian sporting history. It directly led to Brazil changing their kit colors from white to the now-iconic yellow and green, as the white shirts were seen as cursed.

## Legacy
Ghiggia later said, "Only three people have ever silenced the Maracanã: Frank Sinatra, Pope John Paul II, and me." The match remains a defining reference point in South American football culture and is frequently cited as the origin of the term "Maracanazo," which is now used generically in Spanish-language football media to describe any similarly shocking home-soil defeat.
